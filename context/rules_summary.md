# Quadball Rules Summary (current sport, 2023–2026)

Scope: the CURRENT sport of **quadball** (renamed from "quidditch" in 2022). This
covers the three governing rulesets a filmed match is most likely played under:

- **USQ** = US Quadball — *US Quadball Rulebook 2024 v1.0* (held locally) + USQ
  gameplay policies for the 2024‑25 / 2025‑26 seasons.
- **IQA** = International Quadball Association — *IQA Rulebook 2024* (released Aug 2024,
  updated November 2024; current through the 2025 season, a 2026 rulebook is in
  community consultation and NOT yet in force).
- **MLQ** = Major League Quadball — *2024 MLQ Ruleset* (latest confirmed; used for
  the 2024 and 2025 seasons). MLQ maintains its own rulebook closely based on USQ's.

Every fact below is tagged with the ruleset(s) it comes from. Where the three differ,
the difference is called out. See `sources.md` for URLs, dates, and the conflicts list.

> Terminology note (current names, all three rulesets unless noted):
> - "quaffle" → the ball is now called the **quadball** (it is a volleyball).
> - "bludgers" → **dodgeballs** (there are **three**).
> - "snitch" → the **flag**; the "snitch runner" is now the **flag runner**;
>   a "snitch catch" is a **flag catch**.
> - "broom" → still called a **broom** by USQ and MLQ; **IQA renamed it "stick"** in 2024.
>   Players still ride it between the legs while in play.

## 1. Objective & scoring

- **Objective** [USQ/IQA/MLQ]: A mixed‑gender contact sport blending elements of
  rugby, dodgeball, wrestling/handball and flag football. Chasers/keepers try to put
  the quadball through the opponents' hoops; beaters disrupt play with dodgeballs;
  seekers pull the flag from the flag runner.
- **Goal (quadball through a hoop)** = **10 points** [USQ/IQA/MLQ]. Scored through any
  of the three hoops from either side.
- **Flag catch** — this is the biggest divergence between rulesets:
  - **USQ: 35 points.** The catch does **not** end the game; it feeds a "set score"
    (see §4). [USQ Rulebook 4.4.1, 3.4.5]
  - **MLQ: 35 points.** Ends the (flag) period but **not** the game; also feeds a set
    score. [MLQ Scoring / Game Procedures]
  - **IQA: 30 points**, and the catch **ends the game** — *if* the catching team is
    then ahead they win immediately; if they are tied or behind, the game goes to
    overtime (see §4). [IQA Rulebook 2024 via IQA/Wikipedia summaries]
- **When the flag can be caught (seeker floor):** the flag is NOT in play, and there are
  NO seekers on pitch, for the first **20 minutes of game time** (the "seeker floor").
  Confirmed as 20 minutes under **all three** rulesets. (IQA's 2022 rulebook raised it
  from 17 to 20 to match USQ/MLQ.)

## 2. Game length, structure & overtime

- **USQ [Rulebook 3.4]:** Regulation is **two periods separated by a one‑minute
  intermission**, NOT a fixed-length game:
  1. **Seeker floor** = first 20 min of game time (no seekers, no flag).
  2. **Intermission** (1 min): the head referee announces a **set score = 60 points
     above the current leading score** (if tied, 60 above the tied score). Seekers
     report; the flag runner enters.
  3. **Flag period:** play resumes with seekers + flag runner + the set score. Seekers
     are **released 10 seconds into the flag period**. Flag catch = 35 pts (flag runner
     then leaves, seekers become subs). **The game ends when either team reaches the set
     score.** There is **no separate overtime** — the race‑to‑set‑score guarantees a
     winner and makes ties impossible. Game clock is paused on all stoppages.
- **MLQ:** Same set‑score model as USQ — set score = **60 above the leading team's score
  at the end of the first period**; flag catch (35) ends the flag period but not the
  game; **first team to reach/surpass the set score wins.** (Whether MLQ runs any
  additional overtime procedure beyond the set‑score race is not confirmed — the MLQ
  game‑procedures page could not be retrieved; see conflicts list.)
- **IQA:** Traditional flag‑ending model. Flag catch (30) ends the game if the catcher
  is ahead. If the catcher is tied/behind, **overtime**: a set score = **leading team's
  score + 30** is established and the first team to reach it wins; **there are NO seekers
  and NO flag runner during overtime.** Either team may concede during overtime.
- **Typical real‑world match length** [general/Wikipedia]: ~30–40 minutes including
  stoppages, but variable because of the unpredictable flag catch.

## 3. Player roles, counts & headband colors  [USQ/IQA/MLQ — identical]

Each team fields **6 players before the seeker joins, 7 after** (the seeker is the
7th). Roster is **7–21 players** (USQ), rosters of up to **21 athletes** (IQA/MLQ).

| Role | On pitch per team | Headband | What they do |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Chaser** | 3 (part of the 4‑chaser group) | **White** | Handle the quadball; score/defend goals; legal contact. |
| **Keeper** | 1 (is the 4th "chaser") | **Green** | Same as a chaser but has keeper‑zone powers (see §7). |
| **Beater** | 2 | **Black** | Use dodgeballs to "knock out" opponents (see §5). |
| **Seeker** | 1 (only after the seeker floor) | **Yellow** | Pull the flag from the flag runner. |

- USQ phrases it as "**four chasers, one of which is designated the keeper**, and two
  beaters"; IQA/MLQ phrase it as "**one keeper, three chasers, two beaters**" — same six
  players either way.
- The **flag runner** is a **neutral official dressed in yellow/gold**, not a team
  player; job is to avoid capture while staying impartial. [USQ 8.1]

## 4. The four ball types

| Ball | Count | Spec | Role |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Quadball** (the "quaffle") | 1 | A **volleyball**, **25.6–26.4 in circumference**, slightly deflated so a player can grip it one‑handed | Chasers/keepers propel it through hoops for 10 pts [USQ 2.3.1; MLQ same; IQA "slightly‑deflated volleyball"] |
| **Dodgeballs** (the "bludgers") | **3** | **8.5 in diameter, 26.7 in circumference**, slightly deflated (rubber kickball‑type) | Beaters throw/kick them to knock players out [USQ 2.3.2; IQA/MLQ same, "three dodgeballs"] |
| **Flag** (the "snitch") | 1 | A tail with **10–12 in visible length**, affixed to the flag runner's shorts (middle of the back / waistband), yellow/gold shorts | Seekers detach it to score (35 USQ/MLQ, 30 IQA) [USQ 2.3.3, 8.1.2] |

(The three‑dodgeball count is what drives beater strategy — see §5 immunity.)

## 5. The "beat" / knockout mechanic  [USQ terms; IQA/MLQ equivalent]

- Any player struck by an **opponent's live dodgeball** (or knocked off/dismounted from
  their broom) is **"knocked out."** They must immediately: **(a) drop any ball they hold
  and dismount, (b) go back and touch the center (6‑ft) hoop of their own hoops, (c)
  remount before leaving the hoop area**, then may rejoin play. [USQ 5.3.1]
  - A dropped dodgeball becomes dead; a dropped quadball becomes "unscorable."
  - A dodgeball is only "live" while airborne after a beater propels it and before it
    hits the ground/a hoop base/goes out of bounds or is caught. A player hit by a *dead*
    dodgeball is not knocked out.
  - **Headbeats** (hits to the head) are illegal unless a narrow exception applies
    (the struck player changed speed/direction; negligible force; or the ball was thrown
    from ≥ ~6 yards / ~5 m away). Same principle in IQA's 2024 rulebook (its main 2024
    change tightened/clarified headbeat legality). [USQ 5.2.5; IQA 2024]
- **Beater possession dynamics / immunity:** With only **three** dodgeballs and two
  beaters per team, a team can hold at most two; the "third dodgeball" is contested.
  A beater whose team controls two dodgeballs may **claim knockout immunity** (raised
  fist) while going directly to retrieve the third dodgeball — they can't be knocked out
  while doing so, but must proceed straight to the ball, lose immunity once they possess
  it, and can't claim it if the third ball is still live or was already released without
  a real beat attempt. [USQ 5.5]
- A **protected keeper** in their own keeper zone is **immune to knockouts** (see §7).

## 6. Contact, tackling, gender/roster & substitutions

- **Contact** [USQ 6.x; IQA/MLQ substantively similar]: full‑contact but regulated.
  - You may only **wrap/tackle/push/charge a player who is in possession of a ball.**
  - **Push** = one arm only, not from behind, not with the elbow point.
  - **Tackle** = bringing a ball‑carrier to the ground via a wrap; the tackler must
    control both players' momentum (no lifting/slamming = dangerous tackle → red card).
  - Wraps may not be initiated from behind unless the wrapping player is stationary;
    no pulling clothing; no "yanks."
  - Default contact‑foul penalty is a yellow card (back‑to‑hoops for incidental,
    red for violent/dangerous).
  - IQA changed its tackling rules in 2022; broadly aligned in principle with USQ but
    the exact wording lives in the IQA 2024 rulebook (not verified line‑by‑line here).
- **Gender / "maximum same‑gender" rule** — the sport's signature inclusivity rule,
  currently worded as a **maximum on any single gender** ("3 max" / "4 max"):
  - **USQ (Title 9¾ gameplay policy):**
    - **"3 max"** = at most **3 players of a single gender** on pitch during the seeker
      floor, and at most **4 of a single gender** while the flag runner is on the field.
    - **"4 max"** = at most 4 of a single gender at any time in the game.
    - **Which applies is set per division/season, not by the rulebook.** For 2024‑25,
      USQ Cup Club Competitive and College D1 required 3 max. For 2025‑26, USQ Cup 2026
      Club Competitive requires 3 max; **both College divisions use 4 max** (as in
      2023‑24). It is a **pure maximum, gender‑neutral (no minimum).**
  - **IQA (2024 "3‑Max" policy):** During the **seeker floor and overtime**, no more
    than **3 players of the same gender** in play (reduced from 4 in the 2024 rulebook);
    from the **end of the seeker floor until the flag is legally caught**, no more than
    **4 of the same gender**. Pure maximum, gender‑neutral, **no minimum.** A player in
    the penalty box counts as in play; brief violations during a substitution with no
    interaction draw no penalty.
  - **MLQ (2024/2025):** No more than **3 of the same gender** during the seeker floor
    and no more than **4** during seeker‑on‑pitch — **AND, uniquely, a stated minimum of
    "two women" on pitch at all times.** (MLQ is the only one of the three whose current
    public wording includes an explicit minimum‑women clause; see conflicts list re:
    how strictly this is framed.)
- **Substitutions** [USQ 1.3]: **continuous / "on‑the‑fly"** — standard subs are made
  **while play is live**, through the team's substitution area; the exiting player must
  not be knocked out, must leave and dismount out of bounds, and headbands/equipment are
  traded out of bounds. Stopped‑play subs only during timeouts, intermission, injury,
  ejection, penalty‑box position swaps, etc. IQA/MLQ use the same live‑substitution
  model (details not verified line‑by‑line for IQA/MLQ).

## 7. The pitch  [USQ dimensions from Rulebook §2 + local pitch diagram; IQA metric equivalents; MLQ = USQ]

- **Playing pitch:** a **36 × 66 yard** rectangle (36‑yd **endlines**, 66‑yd
  **sidelines**). IQA states the same field as **33 × 60 m**. MLQ: 36 × 66 yd. [USQ 2.1.1;
  IQA; MLQ Equipment page]
- **Player area** (pitch + surrounding run‑off): **48 × 72 yards** [USQ]. Beyond the
  pitch are **substitution areas** (21 × 3 yd, along each team's own half) and **team
  benches** (21 × 3 yd behind them); **penalty boxes** are 6 × 6 yd at the midfield line.
- **Midfield line** across the center; **keeper‑zone lines** **12 yd** either side of
  midfield (so the two keeper zones are **24 yd apart**); **goal lines** **18 yd** either
  side of midfield (hoops sit on the goal lines). [USQ 2.1.2–2.1.5; confirmed by the
  local pitch diagram]
- **Hoops — three per side**, on vertical poles of heights **3 ft, 4.5 ft, 6 ft**
  (≈ **0.91 m / 1.37 m / 1.83 m**) — same heights **all three rulesets**. **Inner loop
  diameter 33 in** [USQ 2.2]. The **6‑ft hoop is centered** on the goal line; the other
  two sit **3 yd (108 in ≈ 2.74 m)** to either side. (Facing the hoops from midfield, the
  3‑ft hoop is on the left, the 4.5‑ft on the right.) *Note: one IQA summary gave hoop
  spacing as ~2.34 m rather than 2.74 m — see conflicts list.*
- **Starting ball positions** (on the midline) [USQ 2.1.6]: the two nearest dodgeballs
  are **6 yd** either side of center; the **third dodgeball and the quadball** are a
  further 3 yd out (**9 yd** from center). The coin toss sets which side the quadball
  starts on. Game starts with "brooms up."
- **Keeper zone** [USQ 7.2]: the area behind a team's own keeper‑zone line around their
  hoops. A **"protected keeper"** (all of their body behind their own keeper‑zone line)
  **cannot be knocked out by dodgeballs** and **cannot be contacted/have the quadball
  stolen** while they solely possess it. These powers switch off once the keeper's team
  moves the live quadball out of the keeper zone, and return when that drive ends.
- **Flag runner / seeker "floor" (release timing)** [USQ 8.2, 3.4.5]:
  - The **flag runner** is released onto the pitch **after the 20‑minute seeker floor
    ends** (at the intermission).
  - **Seekers** are released **10 seconds into the flag period** (USQ). A seeker who
    enters early commits a false start (blue card).
  - Once seekers are released, the flag runner must stay **between the keeper‑zone lines**
    and within ~3 steps of the boundary, and must not show bias, leave the pitch, or try
    to be "downed."

## KEY DIFFERENCES AT A GLANCE

| Topic | USQ | IQA | MLQ |
|---|---|---|---|
| Flag catch value | **35 pts** | **30 pts** | **35 pts** |
| Does the flag catch end the game? | **No** — feeds a set score | **Yes** (ends game; win if ahead, else OT) | **No** — ends flag period, feeds a set score |
| Ending format | Race to **set score = leader + 60**; no separate OT | Flag ends game; **OT set score = leader + 30**, no seekers/flag in OT | Race to **set score = leader + 60** (any extra OT unconfirmed) |
| Seeker floor | 20 min | 20 min | 20 min |
| Seeker release | 10 s into flag period | after seeker floor (traditional) | after seeker floor |
| Gender rule | 3‑max / 4‑max, **pure maximum**, by division | 3‑max (floor & OT) / 4‑max, **pure maximum** | 3‑max / 4‑max **plus stated minimum "two women"** |
| "Broom" name | broom | **stick** (2024) | broom |
| Field | 36 × 66 yd | 33 × 60 m (same field) | 36 × 66 yd |
| Roles, counts, headbands, hoops (3/4.5/6 ft), balls (1 quadball + 3 dodgeballs + flag) | — identical across all three — | | |

*Gaps / things not fully verified are listed in `sources.md`.*
