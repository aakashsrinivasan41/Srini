# Quadball Terminology (current era, 2023–2026)

Glossary of terms a live commentator, player, or referee uses in **quadball** (the sport
renamed from "quidditch" in 2022). Definitions are for the CURRENT game, not the fantasy
version.

**Primary source:** *US Quadball Rulebook 2024, Version 1.0* — the ruleset used for USQ
official games in the 2024–25 season. Held locally at
`/home/user/Srini/context/US_Quadball_Rulebook_2024_v1.0.pdf` (extracted text:
`rulebook.txt`). Section numbers below (e.g. §5.3.1) refer to that rulebook.
Ruleset-specific terms are tagged **[USQ]** (US Quadball), **[IQA]** (International Quadball
Association, 2024 rulebook), or **[MLQ]** (Major League Quadball). Terms with no tag are
common to all three current rulesets. Items marked **[commentary]** are broadcast/community
slang, not rulebook wording. Web items accessed 2026-07-17.

> **Naming note for film work:** the rulebook now calls the red ball "the **Quadball**" and the
> dodgeballs "**dodgeballs**." Players and commentators still overwhelmingly say "**quaffle**"
> and "**bludger**." Both are in live use; treat them as synonyms.

## The positions (who's who)

- **Chaser** — One of the three (non-keeper) attackers who handle the Quadball/quaffle to score
  10-point goals. **White headband.** [USQ §1.2.2, Appendix A]
- **Keeper** — The fourth "chaser," the goal defender. **Green headband.** Immune to knockouts
  and has uncontestable possession while inside their own keeper zone ("protected keeper").
  [USQ §7.2.2]
- **Beater** — One of two players who throw dodgeballs/bludgers to "knock out" opponents.
  **Black headband.** [USQ §1.2.2]
- **Seeker** — The player who chases the flag runner to pull the flag. **Yellow headband.** Only
  on the pitch during the flag period. [USQ §1.2.2]
- **Speaking captain** — The one player allowed to speak to officials for the team. [USQ §1.1.1]

## The balls

- **Quaffle / Quadball** — The scoring ball; a slightly-deflated **volleyball** (25.6–26.4 in
  circumference). Handled only by chasers/keepers. A goal through a hoop = **10 points**.
  Officially "the Quadball"; "quaffle" is the legacy/colloquial name. [USQ §2.3.1]
- **Bludger / dodgeball** — One of **three** slightly-deflated rubber **dodgeballs** (8.5 in
  diameter). Handled only by beaters; used to knock out opponents. [USQ §2.3.2]
- **Flag / flag tail** — A **tennis ball inside a fabric tail (sleeve), 10–12 in visible length**,
  attached to the **middle of the back** of the flag runner's shorts. Pulling it = the "flag
  catch." Worth **35 points [USQ/MLQ]** / **30 points [IQA]** (see Flag catch). [USQ §2.3.3,
  §4.4.1]

## Beater / dodgeball play

- **Beat** — To hit an opponent with a live dodgeball (verb). "He got the beat." [USQ §5.2]
- **Beaten / knocked out** — A player struck by an opponent's **live** dodgeball is "knocked out":
  they must immediately drop any ball, dismount, and do the knockout procedure. [USQ §5.2.1]
- **Knockout procedure** — The required reset after a beat: drop ball → dismount → **touch your
  team's center (6-ft) hoop** → remount, then rejoin. [USQ §5.3.1]
- **"Beat!"** — Referee's verbal call confirming a player IS knocked out. [USQ §5.2.4]
- **"Safe" / "Clear"** — Referee's verbal call that a struck player is NOT knocked out (e.g. hit
  by a dead ball or their own team's ball); play continues. The **[commentary]** word "**good**"
  is used loosely for the same "you're safe / that didn't count" idea; the rulebook words are
  "safe" and "clear." [USQ §5.2.4]
- **Live dodgeball** — A dodgeball is "live" (can knock out) once a beater propels it, until it
  hits the ground/a hoop base, goes out of bounds, is caught, or is stripped. Otherwise it is
  **dead**. [USQ §5.2.2]
- **Bludger control / dodgeball control / "dodgeball supremacy"** — Holding **2 of the 3**
  dodgeballs, the standing tactical advantage (2 beaters, 3 balls). **[commentary]** term; the
  rulebook mechanic behind it is the "third dodgeball." [USQ §5.5.1; web 2026-07-17]
- **Third dodgeball** — When one team holds two dodgeballs and the last one is loose and dead, it
  becomes the "third dodgeball," which triggers immunity rules. [USQ §5.5.1]
- **Knockout immunity / "fist up"** — A beater on the team with **no** dodgeballs may raise a
  closed fist above the shoulder to become immune while going to collect the third dodgeball.
  On video: a **beater running with one fist raised**. [USQ §5.5.2]
- **"No bludger"** — **[commentary]** Call/observation that the ball-carrier is NOT under threat
  of a bludger (the defending beater has no ball / is out of position), so the offense can drive
  freely. [web 2026-07-17; rulebook concept, not a defined term]
- **Bludger exchange** — **[commentary/tactical]** A beater passing/handing a dodgeball to their
  teammate beater (e.g. after committing to a beat) so a fresh ball can defend. The rulebook
  simply allows "beaters may pass any dodgeball to a friendly beater." [USQ §7.1.1; web 2026-07-17]
- **Struck beater** — A beater who was hit but may try to CATCH the ball that hit them before it
  goes dead; if they catch it they stay in. [USQ §5.4.3]
- **Headbeat** — A dodgeball whose first contact is an opponent's head/neck; generally illegal.
  [USQ §5.2.5]

## Chaser / quaffle play

- **Drive** — An offensive possession/push toward the hoops. A team "on a drive" is the offensive
  team; the drive ends when the other team gets the Quadball, a goal is scored, or the period
  ends. Also used as a verb: "the keeper drives up the middle." [USQ §7.3.1]
- **Dunk** — **[commentary, informal — UNCERTAIN as official term]** Scoring at very close range
  by putting the Quadball through the hoop by hand rather than shooting from distance. Not defined
  in the USQ rulebook; treat as broadcast slang.
- **Goal / "good goal"** — Whole Quadball passes through a hoop (from either side) = 10 points,
  confirmed by the head referee. [USQ §4.1.1]
- **Goaltending** — A non-keeper chaser touching the Quadball from the far side of a hoop; scored
  as if it went through. [USQ §4.1.2]
- **Keeper zone** — The area behind a team's keeper-zone line (containing that team's hoops)
  where the keeper is knockout-immune and cannot be contested for the ball. [USQ §7.2, §2.1.3]
- **"On hoops" / "return to hoops" / "back to hoops"** — Sending a player back to touch their own
  hoops to reset — either after a knockout, or as the minor penalty literally named **"back to
  hoops."** The knockout procedure requires touching the **center (6-ft) hoop**. Commentators say
  a knocked-out player is "going back to hoops" / "tapping in on hoops." [USQ §5.3.1, §9.1.3]
- **Reset** — Legally moving the Quadball backward across the midfield line; allowed once per
  drive. Ref calls **"reset used."** [USQ §7.4.3]
- **Restrictor lines** — The keeper-zone line (hard) and midfield line (soft) that limit carrying
  the ball backward. [USQ §7.4.3]
- **Stall / stalled Quadball** — A ball-carrier held on the ground in contact; a 10-count leads to
  a turnover to the defending keeper. [USQ §7.4.2]

## Seeker / flag play

- **Flag runner** — The **neutral official in yellow** who wears the flag and evades both seekers;
  also an on-pitch official who can make certain calls. [USQ §8, §10.1.9]
- **Seeker floor** — The **first 20 minutes** of the game, during which there are NO seekers and
  the flag cannot be caught. (Historical "18-minute" figures are outdated — current USQ/IQA/MLQ
  all use 20 min.) [USQ §3.4.3, Appendix A; web 2026-07-17]
- **Flag period** — The second period, after intermission, once seekers and the flag runner are
  added. Seekers are released **10 seconds into** the flag period. [USQ §3.4.5]
- **Live flag** — **[commentary]** The flag is "live" / in play, i.e. catchable, once the flag
  period has started and the runner is not down. [USQ §8.4.1]
- **Flag pull / flag catch / "the catch"** — A seeker cleanly detaching the flag from the runner.
  Officially "**flag catch**." Worth **35 points [USQ/MLQ]** / **30 points [IQA]**. [USQ §4.4.1;
  web 2026-07-17]
- **"Good" (catch)** — Referee confirmation that a flag catch (or goal) counts; opposite is
  **"no good."** [USQ §2.3.4, §4.4]
- **Down / downed flag runner** — The flag is uncatchable when the runner is "down" (any body part
  other than hands/feet on the ground, or out of bounds, or after a stoppage). Downed runner gets
  a **3-second head start** before seekers may re-pursue. [USQ §8.4.1, §8.4.3]

## Brooms

- **Broom** — A rigid plastic pole **39–41 in** long that every in-play player must keep between
  their legs. Not attached to the body. (Commentators still say "broom"; it's just a pipe.)
  [USQ §2.4.1]
- **Mount / "brooms up"** — To have the broom between the legs = "mounted" = in play. **"Brooms
  up!"** is the head referee's start-of-play call (play begins on the first "B" sound). [USQ
  §5.1.1, §3.2.1]
- **"Brooms down!"** — Pre-start call; after it, players may not change position/location. [USQ §3.2.1]
- **Dismount** — Broom no longer between the legs. A player who becomes dismounted in play is
  **immediately knocked out.** On video: broom drops / player pulls it out from between the legs.
  [USQ §5.1.1–5.1.2]
- **"Remount!"** — Head referee's call to re-mount before a restart whistle. [USQ §3.3.3]

## Game structure & scoring

- **Set score / score cap** — At intermission the head referee sets a target of **+60 points over
  the leading score**; the first team to reach it ends the game. [USQ §3.4.4]
- **Intermission** — One-minute break between the seeker floor and flag period; the flag runner
  enters and seekers report during it. [USQ §3.4.4]

## Officiating & penalties

- **Head referee (HR)** — The lead official; follows the Quadball, confirms goals, and is the
  **only** official who may issue cards. [USQ §10.1.1]
- **Assistant referee (AR) / lead AR** — Officials for dodgeball/off-ball play: they make the
  **beat and safe calls**, out-of-bounds, and advise the HR. (These are effectively the "bludger
  refs.") [USQ §10.1.4]
- **Seeker referee / flag referee** — Watches the flag runner–seeker interaction, rules the runner
  down, and counts the 3-second head start. [USQ §10.1.5]
- **Goal judge** — Watches shots at the hoops and endline out-of-bounds. [USQ §10.1.6]
- **Back to hoops** — Lowest penalty: player must redo the knockout procedure. [USQ §9.1.3]
- **Turnover** — Possession of a specific ball handed to the other team. [USQ §9.1.4]
- **Blue card** — 1 minute in the penalty box, team plays down; released early if opponents score;
  does **not** stack. Technical/procedural fouls. [USQ §9.1.5, §9.4.1]
- **Yellow card** — 1 minute in the box, team plays down; released early on an opponent's goal;
  **three yellows = a red.** Default contact-foul penalty. [USQ §9.1.5, §6.2.1]
- **Red card** — Player **ejected**; a substitute serves **2 full minutes** that scores cannot
  shorten. [USQ §9.1.5, §9.4.1]
- **Delayed penalty** — A foul seen by a non-HR official, who **raises a hand** and lets play
  continue until it's advantageous to stop. [USQ §9.6]
- **Advantage** — HR lets play continue after a foul (throws a location marker + raises one arm)
  rather than stopping immediately. [USQ §9.5.1]
- **"Ball out"** — HR's call that a wrapped ball-carrier has released the ball, so the wrap must
  stop. [USQ §6.3.1]
- **"Boundary"** — HR's call that the Quadball has gone out of bounds. [USQ §7.5.2]

### Source-conflict flags
- **Flag catch value:** **35 points** in the US (USQ Rulebook 2024 §4.4.1; MLQ) vs. **30 points**
  internationally (IQA 2024 rulebook / Wikipedia, web 2026-07-17). Confirm which ruleset the film
  is from before trusting a scoreboard jump.
- **Seeker floor length:** current rulesets = **20 minutes** (USQ Rulebook 2024 §3.4.3). Older/web
  references citing **18 minutes** (or "snitch at 17, seekers at 18") are **outdated** — do not use.
- **Official vs. colloquial ball names:** rulebook = "Quadball" / "dodgeball"; live commentary =
  "quaffle" / "bludger." Not a real conflict, just parallel usage.
- **Direct fetch of usquadball.org / iqasport.org / mlquadball.com was blocked by network egress
  policy on 2026-07-17;** the local USQ 2024 rulebook PDF is the authoritative primary source used
  above, with web-search summaries used only to corroborate IQA/MLQ differences.
