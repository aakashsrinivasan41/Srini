## 1. The game in one screen (current quadball, 2023–2026)

- Per team on pitch: **4 chasers (1 is the keeper) + 2 beaters**, and **1 seeker only
  after the seeker floor**. Headbands: **green = keeper, white = chaser, black = beater,
  yellow = seeker**. Officials wear **black/white vertical stripes**.
- Balls: **1 quaffle** (a volleyball — chasers/keeper score through hoops, 10 pts) +
  **3 dodgeballs** (rubber "bludgers" — beaters use them to "knock out" opponents) +
  **1 flag** (tennis ball in a tail on the neutral yellow **flag runner**; seeker pulls
  it — **30 pts & ends the game in IQA**, **35 pts + set score in USQ/MLQ**).
- Structure: **seeker floor = first 20 min** (no seekers/flag). Then a 1-min intermission,
  a **set score** is announced, and the **flag period** adds seekers + flag runner.
- **Knockout ("back to hoops"):** any player hit by an opponent's live dodgeball must drop
  their ball, dismount, run and **touch the center 6-ft hoop**, remount, then rejoin.
- **Keeper zone:** a keeper in their own zone is a **protected keeper** — dodgeball-immune
  and can't be contacted while holding the quaffle — until a teammate carries the ball out
  of the zone.
- Pitch: 36×66 yd; three hoops per end (**3 / 4.5 / 6 ft**, 6-ft in the center; facing
  from midfield the 3-ft is on the **left**, 4.5-ft on the **right**); keeper-zone lines
  12 yd from midfield, goal lines 18 yd.

## 2. Concepts most relevant to reading a possession

- **Watch the dodgeballs before the quaffle.** "**Control**" = the team holding **2 of 3
  dodgeballs**. Control decides whether chasers can drive or must wait.
- **Offenses drive into a "no-bludger window."** A carrier attacks the hoops when the
  defensive beater has no live dodgeball to beat them; offenses manufacture that window
  (draw the beat, or send an extra beater up).
- **Keeper = a 4th chaser on offense** and a safe launch point (protected in the zone).
- **A drive ends the instant the other team gains the quaffle** → possession and the
  offense/defense labels flip immediately. Turnovers → fast breaks before defenses reset.
- **Hoop defense:** chasers pack around their own hoops; the keeper anchors. To finish, the
  offense must clear/occupy the defensive beater and a defender.

## 3. What real tape taught me (beyond the paper rules)

*From Clip 1 (India–Mexico, IQA WC 2023) — first tape studied:*
- **The bludger fight literally follows the ball-carrier down the pitch.** On a fast break
  the two teams' beaters contest one dodgeball *in the middle of the running pack*, right
  next to the quaffle-carrier — you can see "control" being decided in real time as a
  physical scramble, not an abstract stat.
- **Turnovers at the hoops are chaotic and fast.** Possession flipped inside a collapsing
  pile at the defending team's hoops and was sprinting the other way within ~1–2 seconds.
  On wide footage the *mechanism* (save vs rebound vs strip vs beat) is often invisible even
  when the *result* (who has the ball) is clear.
- **Seeker-floor endgame looks like end-to-end transition**, not patient half-court sets —
  at least in this possession.
- **Reading balls needs zoom.** On a wide screen-recorded stream, the quaffle (white
  volleyball, carried under-arm) is only separable from the orange dodgeball at heavy
  crop+upscale. Position headbands are mostly below the resolution limit.

## 4. Terminology I can now recognize *visually*

- **Quaffle vs dodgeball on video:** quaffle = **white/light volleyball, carried under the
  arm or two-handed by a chaser**; dodgeball = **bright (orange here) rubber ball, thrown/
  held by a beater**, ~⅓ smaller. Tell them apart by **who holds it + size/color**, never
  color alone.
- **Fast break / transition:** a **mixed pack sprinting together** one direction with the
  carrier inside it and a bludger contested in the middle.
- **Hoop defense:** a **cluster of one team's jerseys standing around their own three
  hoops** as the other team drives in.
- **Knockout ("back to hoops"):** *not yet seen cleanly on tape* — look for a player
  dropping a ball, stepping off their broom, and jogging to tag the center hoop.
