## Interpretation through the current rules (why it looks the way it does)

- **Why it's frantic, end-to-end, no set "keeper restart":** this is **open-play
  transition**, not a possession that began from a keeper restart after a goal. A drive
  ends the instant the other team gains possession (USQ 7.3.1 / IQA equiv.), so the
  turnover at Mexico's hoops (Phase C) **immediately started Mexico's drive** and India
  became the defense — hence the instant flip and the sprint the other way.
- **Why beaters fight over the dodgeball during the break (Phase D):** bludger **"control"
  (holding 2 of 3 dodgeballs)** is the master variable (see `context/tactics.md`).
  Mexico's ball-carrier can only be stopped by a **beat**; whichever beater wins that
  orange dodgeball in the middle decides whether the break is **beaten dead or protected
  through**. That's why the ball-fight tracks the quaffle-carrier down the pitch.
- **Why India crashed the hoops with beater support (Phase B):** to finish a drive at the
  hoops a chaser generally needs the defensive beater neutralized ("no-bludger window").
  India bringing the orange dodgeball to the hoop line is consistent with trying to
  manufacture that window; Mexico's **hoop defense** (chasers packed at their own hoops)
  held.
- **Why there's no seeker/flag action:** game clock < 20:00 means the **seeker floor** is
  still on — seekers and the flag runner have not entered (USQ 3.4.3). Everything here is
  chaser + beater play, which is exactly what we see.
- **The yellow-shirted person:** because seekers/flag runner are **not legal in the seeker
  floor**, a yellow figure moving with play is most consistent with an **assistant/field
  referee in a yellow officiating shirt**, not a player. **[INF, flagged — Q3.]**
