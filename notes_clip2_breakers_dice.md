**CONCEPTS these situations touch (from `context/tactics.md`, USQ-general, NOT judgment)**
- To finish a drive at the hoops, the offense usually needs the **defending beater
  neutralized** — a "no-bludger window." An offensive beater with a bludger typically
  works **near/ahead of the ball-carrier** to threaten the defensive beater or force/eat a
  beat, opening the shooter's lane. Beaters parked on the weak-side flank away from the
  ball don't create that window.
- On defense at your own hoops, a beater with **bludger control** guarding the hoop zone
  lets chasers play the ball-carrier more aggressively (a beat behind them cleans up a
  driver). Watching whether the Breakers' beater had a live bludger when #6 drove would
  explain whether the wrap attempt was "supported" or on an island.

## USQ rules interpretation touchpoints
- **Wrap/tackle (Clip B):** a **wrap** = encircling an opponent with an arm; a **tackle** =
  a wrap that brings them to the ground (USQ §6.3.1–6.3.2). Legal only on a player **in
  possession of a ball**, and **not from behind with momentum**. The user "trying to wrap
  but not completing" is a normal, legal defensive attempt if #6 had the quaffle and the
  wrap wasn't from behind.
- **Losing the ball at the hoops (Clip A):** a drive ends the instant the defense gains
  possession (§7.3.1); a blocked shot that the defense recovers is an immediate turnover →
  Breakers become defense (which is exactly the Clip A → Clip B story, IF the clips are
  contiguous — unconfirmed).
- **No keeper-zone protection abuse seen:** ordinary open-play offense/defense.
